The Equation of Motion

Unifying resistance and compliance

Building the Equation of Motion

Driving pressure overcomes two opposing forces, plus any baseline pressure already present:

Pvent + Pmuscle  =  (Flow × R)  +  (Volume / C)  +  PEEP

Resistive pressure

Flow × R — overcomes friction in the airway/circuit.

Elastic pressure

Volume / C — overcomes elastic recoil.

PEEP

Baseline pressure already in the system.

Passive patient (Pmuscle=0): Pvent alone equals this sum.

Section D

The Time Constant

τ = R × C

What is the Time Constant?

τ (seconds) = Resistance × Compliance

τ describes how quickly the lung fills or empties passively along an exponential curve. It matters most for expiration: if Te is shorter than the lung needs, gas is trapped and the next breath stacks on top of it.

  • After 1τ, ~63% of the volume change has occurred
  • After 3τ, ~95% — a common rule-of-thumb target for adequate emptying
  • After 5τ, ~99% — considered essentially complete

Section E

Mechanical Power

From a single breath to energy delivered per minute

From Pressure-Volume Loop to Power

The area enclosed by the pressure-volume loop for one breath is the mechanical energy delivered that breath. Multiply by respiratory rate and you get mechanical power — energy delivered per minute.

Power (J/min) ≈ Energy per breath (J) × Respiratory Rate (/min)

Higher power has been associated with ventilator-induced lung injury in observational studies — it is a way of totalling how much mechanical stress the lung receives per minute, not just per breath.

Discussion Case

A passive, volume-controlled patient: constant flow 0.8 L/s, VT 480 mL, PEEP 5 cmH₂O.
Ppeak = 28 cmH₂O, Pplat = 20 cmH₂O (measured with an end-inspiratory pause).
  • What is the resistive pressure? What is R?
  • What is the elastic pressure? What is C?
  • If Te available is 1.0 s, is that adequate relative to τ?
  • At a respiratory rate of 18/min, roughly what is the mechanical power?
  • Estimate Paw for this breath (assume RR 18/min, I:E 1:2, ascending-ramp waveform).
  • Dead space is ≈150 mL. What is the alveolar ventilation at RR 18/min, and what is VD/VT?

Work through this as a group — every quantity here was covered in the interactive slides above.

Case Answers

1 · Resistance

R = (Ppeak−Pplat)/Flow = 8/0.8 = 10 cmH₂O/L/s

2 · Compliance

C = VT/(Pplat−PEEP) = 480/15 ≈ 32 mL/cmH₂O

3 · Time Constant

τ = R×C = 0.32 s → 3τ = 0.96 s ≤ Te(1.0s): adequate

4 · Mech. Power

ΔP = Pplat−PEEP = 15 cmH₂O → MP ≈ 17.4 J/min

5 · Mean Paw

Ti/TCT = ⅓ (I:E 1:2) → Paw ≈ 8.8 cmH₂O

6 · Dead Space

V̇A = (480−150)×18/1000 ≈ 5.9 L/min; VD/VT ≈ 0.31
